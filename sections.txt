  "Isotopes": "<style data-mw-deduplicate=\"TemplateStyles:r1368532237\">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+.mw-empty-elt+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}@media print{body.ns-0 .mw-parser-output .hatnote{display:none!important}}</style><div role=\"note\" class=\"hatnote navigation-not-searchable\">Main article: Isotopes of <a href=\"/school1/ptable/element/Md\" class=\"wiki-link\" title=\"Mendelevium (Md)\">mendelevium</a></div>\n<p>Seventeen isotopes of <a href=\"/school1/ptable/element/Md\" class=\"wiki-link\" title=\"Mendelevium (Md)\">mendelevium</a> are known, with mass numbers from 244 to 260; all are radioactive. The longest-lived isotope is <sup>258</sup>Md with a half-life of 51.6&#160;days. Nevertheless, the shorter-lived <sup>256</sup>Md (half-life 77.7&#160;minutes) is more often used in chemical experiments because it can be produced in larger quantities from <a href=\"/school1/ptable/element/Es\" class=\"wiki-link\" title=\"Einsteinium (Es)\">einsteinium</a>, as <sup>258</sup>Md would require <sup>255</sup>Es, of which significant quantities are available only as a minor component of an isotopic mixture.\n</p><p>The half-lives of <a href=\"/school1/ptable/element/Md\" class=\"wiki-link\" title=\"Mendelevium (Md)\">mendelevium</a> isotopes mostly increase smoothly (apart from odd/even effects) toward higher mass, up to <sup>258</sup>Md, then decrease (as indicated by what experimental data is available) as spontaneous fission becomes the dominant decay mode; the second longest-living isotope is <sup>260</sup>Md, the heaviest known, with a half-life of 27.8&#160;days. Mendelevium is the last element that has any known isotope with a half-life longer than a day.\n</p><p>Mendelevium-256, the currently most important isotope of mendelevium, decays about 90% through electron capture and 10% through alpha decay. It is most easily detected through the spontaneous fission of its electron capture daughter <a href=\"/school1/ptable/element/Fm\" class=\"wiki-link\" title=\"Fermium (Fm)\">fermium</a>-256, but in the presence of other nuclides that undergo spontaneous fission, alpha decays at the characteristic energies for mendelevium-256 (7.205 and 7.139&#160;MeV) can provide more useful identification.\n</p>\n<div class=\"mw-heading mw-heading2\">"
