<p>There are 19 known <a href="/school1/ptable/element/Am" class="wiki-link" title="Americium (Am)">americium</a> isotopes and 11 nuclear isomers, having mass numbers 229 through 247. There are two long-lived alpha-emitters; <sup>243</sup>Am has a half-life of 7,350&#160;years and is the most stable isotope, and <sup>241</sup>Am has a half-life of 432.6&#160;years. The most stable nuclear isomer is <sup>242m1</sup>Am – generally called simply <sup>242m</sup>Am – with a long half-life of 141&#160;years. The half-lives of other isotopes and isomers are much shorter with a maximum of 50.8&#160;hours for <sup>240</sup>Am. As with most other actinides, the isotopes of <a href="/school1/ptable/element/Am" class="wiki-link" title="Americium (Am)">americium</a> with odd number of neutrons have relatively high fissionability with thermal neutrons and low critical mass.
</p><p>Americium-241 decays to <sup>237</sup>Np emitting alpha particles of several different energies, mostly at 5.486&#160;MeV (85.2%) and 5.443&#160;MeV (12.8%). Because the resulting states are metastable, gamma rays are also emitted at discrete energies between 26.3 and 158.5&#160;keV, by far the strongest is at 59.5&#160;keV.
</p><p>The ground state of Americium-242 is a short-lived isotope with a half-life of 16.02&#160;h. It mostly (82.7%) converts by β-decay to <sup>242</sup>Cm, but also by electron capture to <sup>242</sup>Pu (17.3%). 
</p><p>Nearly all (99.55%) of the metastable <sup>242m</sup>Am decays by internal conversion to <sup>242</sup>Am and the remaining 0.45% by α-decay to <sup>238</sup>Np. 
</p><p>Americium-243 transforms by α-emission into <sup>239</sup>Np.
</p>
