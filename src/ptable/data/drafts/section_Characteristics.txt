<p><a href="/school1/ptable/element/Au" class="wiki-link" title="Gold (Au)">Gold</a> is the most malleable metal. It can be drawn into a wire of single-atom width, and then stretched considerably before it breaks. Such nanowires distort via the formation, reorientation, and migration of dislocations and crystal twins without noticeable hardening. A single gram of gold can be beaten into a sheet of 1 square meter (11&#160;sq&#160;ft), and an avoirdupois ounce into 28 square meters (300&#160;sq&#160;ft). Gold leaf can be beaten thin enough to become semi-transparent. Light transmitted through gold appears greenish-blue, because gold strongly reflects yellow and red. Such semi-transparent sheets also strongly reflect infrared light, making them useful as infrared (radiant heat) shields in the visors of heat-resistant suits and in sun visors for spacesuits. Gold is a good conductor of heat and electricity.
</p><p>Gold has a density of 19.3&#160;g/cm<sup>3</sup>, almost identical to that of <a href="/school1/ptable/element/W" class="wiki-link" title="Tungsten (W)">tungsten</a> at 19.25&#160;g/cm<sup>3</sup>; as such, <a href="/school1/ptable/element/W" class="wiki-link" title="Tungsten (W)">tungsten</a> has been used in the counterfeiting of gold bars, such as by plating a <a href="/school1/ptable/element/W" class="wiki-link" title="Tungsten (W)">tungsten</a> bar with gold. By comparison, the density of <a href="/school1/ptable/element/Pb" class="wiki-link" title="Lead (Pb)">lead</a> is 11.34&#160;g/cm<sup>3</sup>, and that of the densest element, <a href="/school1/ptable/element/Os" class="wiki-link" title="Osmium (Os)">osmium</a>, is <span class="nowrap"><span data-sort-value="7001225880000000000♠"></span>22.588<span style="margin-left:0.225em;margin-right:0.225em;">±</span>0.015&#160;g/cm<sup>3</sup></span>.
</p>
