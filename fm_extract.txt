<p><b>Fermium</b> is a synthetic chemical element; it has symbol <b>Fm</b> and atomic number 100. It is an actinide and the heaviest element that can be formed by neutron bombardment of lighter elements, and hence the last element that can be prepared in macroscopic quantities, although pure fermium metal has not been  prepared yet. A total of 20 isotopes are known, with <sup>257</sup>Fm being the longest-lived with a half-life of 100.5 days.
</p><p>Fermium was discovered in the debris of the first <a href="/school1/ptable/element/H" class="wiki-link" title="Hydrogen (H)">hydrogen</a> bomb explosion in 1952, and named after Enrico Fermi, one of the pioneers of nuclear physics. Its chemistry is typical for the late actinides, with a preponderance of the +3 oxidation state but also an accessible +2 oxidation state. Owing to the small amounts of produced fermium and all of its isotopes having relatively short half-lives, there are currently no uses for it outside basic scientific research.
</p>
